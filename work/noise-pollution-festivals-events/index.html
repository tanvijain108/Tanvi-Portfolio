Project overview

A research-driven design project exploring how noise from festivals and events impacts health, daily life, and urban communities, aiming to create balanced solutions between cultural celebration and community well-being.

Project credits

Created by: Event / Festival

Mentored by: FestivalWorks

RESEARCH & DISCOVERY

In this project, we conducted primary research through interviews and surveys to understand how noise from festivals and events impacts people’s daily lives, health, and environment. We interacted with key stakeholders like residents, police, doctors, and shopkeepers to gather diverse real-world perspectives.

The research revealed patterns such as frequent exposure to loud noise, peak disturbance during evenings and nights, and strong effects on vulnerable groups like children, elderly, and students. These insights helped us build a clear understanding of the problem context.

PROBLEM DEFINITION

After analyzing the research data, we identified major issues including health impacts, ineffective complaint systems, and challenges faced by authorities in enforcing noise regulations. There was also a clear gap between awareness and actual behavior during festivals.

We framed a problem statement focusing on the conflict between cultural celebrations and community well-being, highlighting the need for a balanced approach that respects traditions while minimizing negative impacts on society.

IDEATION & SOLUTION DESIGN

Based on the defined problem, we explored multiple solution directions such as awareness systems, improved communication channels, and better coordination between stakeholders. The focus was on creating solutions that are practical, inclusive, and culturally sensitive.

We developed a concept that aims to reduce noise-related issues through structured systems and behavioral change, ensuring that festivals can be celebrated responsibly without compromising people’s health and daily life.

TESTING & KEY INSIGHTS

We evaluated our ideas through user feedback and usability thinking to understand how effective and acceptable the solutions are in real-world scenarios. This helped us identify gaps in usability, accessibility, and user trust.

The testing phase highlighted the importance of simple reporting systems, early planning, and awareness. It reinforced that solving noise pollution requires not just rules, but better communication, collaboration, and user-centered design.
